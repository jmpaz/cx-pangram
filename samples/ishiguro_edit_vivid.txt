To read Kazuo Ishiguro is to be frustrated on every conceivable level. The peculiar genius of his maddening prose is that frustration blooms no matter how deeply—or how little—the reader chooses to invest in his characters and their fates. On the page itself, one meets circling repetition, a studied long-windedness, and qualifying adjectives scattered like seeds across the soil of every sentence. Ishiguro has trained me to wince each time a narrator murmurs some variation of "Let me be brief." His narrators all hold jobs, yet not one is a storyteller by trade. Facts arrive slowly, imprecisely, shuffled out of sequence—withholding the very details a reader needs to grasp what is actually unfolding.
