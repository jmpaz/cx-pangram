There is, I have come to feel, a particular species of frustration reserved for the reader of Kazuo Ishiguro—though I confess I find it difficult to say precisely when I first became aware of it. What strikes me, looking back, is that the frustration does not seem to depend upon one's attachment to the characters at all; it abides whether one cares a great deal or hardly cares at all. The language circles, and qualifies, and returns upon itself. The narrators, employed though they are, are not the sort of people accustomed to telling stories, and so what we are given comes to us slowly, a little out of order, and never quite complete—as though the facts that might have steadied us had been, with some quiet deliberation, kept just beyond our reach.
